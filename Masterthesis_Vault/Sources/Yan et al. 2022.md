# [[Yan et al. 2022.pdf|A Multifaceted benchmarking of synthetic electronic health record generation models]]

### Research Gap:

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=1&annotation=941R|Yan et al. 2022, p.1]])
> Yet there is a lack of a systematic assessment framework to benchmark methods as they emerge and determine which methods are most appropriate for which use cases.

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=943R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> First, there is a lack of consensus on the evaluation metrics that should be applied to assess synthetic EHR data.

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=945R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> the comparisons are not systematic and are susceptible to the self-assessment trap19 in that the model developers benchmark their own models.

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=947R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> Most publications on EHR data synthesis neglect the use case, such that it is unclear what conditions are ideal for the simulation model.


### Methodik:

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=949R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> we introduce a benchmarking framework to evaluate [[GAN]] models for EHR synthesis

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=951R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> The benchmarking framework focuses on two perspectives—utility and privacy

Utility:
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=953R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> Resemblance focuses on the internal distributional characteristics of data
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=4&annotation=965R|Yan et al. 2022, p.4]])
> Proportion of generated records that violate clinical knowledge derived from the real dataset

Privacy:
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=955R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> Data privacy was assessed through four metrics

Models:
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=957R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> We used this framework to evaluate five EHR synthetic data generation models based on GANs8: (1) medGAN25, (2) medBGAN35, (3) EMR-WGAN20, (4) WGAN35, and (5) DPGAN36.

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=959R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> we performed model training and synthetic data generation five times for each model.

Datasets:
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=4&annotation=961R|Yan et al. 2022, p.4]])
> We performed our benchmarking using EHR data from two large academic medical centers in the United States, the University of Washington (UW) and Vanderbilt University Medical Center (VUMC).

3 Use Cases:
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=6&annotation=963R|Yan et al. 2022, p.6]])
> (1) education, (2) medical AI development, and (3) system development


### Main Finding:

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=1&annotation=967R|Yan et al. 2022, p.1]])
> The results illustrate that there is a utility-privacy tradeoff for sharing synthetic health data and further indicate that no method is unequivocally the best on all criteria in each use case

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=8&annotation=969R|Yan et al. 2022, p.8]])
> EMR-WGAN consistently performs well for the majority of utility metrics

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=8&annotation=971R|Yan et al. 2022, p.8]])
> there is not much benefit in incorporating differential privacy into the synthetic data generation process.

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=10&annotation=973R|Yan et al. 2022, p.10]])
> We observed that all models induced some level of knowledge violation
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=10&annotation=975R|Yan et al. 2022, p.10]])
> over 50% of the synthetic records with the prostate cancer diagnosis code generated by DPGAN are associated with a generated Female gender.
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=10&annotation=977R|Yan et al. 2022, p.10]])
> This phenomenon implies that GAN models are unable to perfectly recognize and learn from the record-level knowledge in EHR.


### Limitation:

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=11&annotation=979R|Yan et al. 2022, p.11]])
> the evaluation metrics we incorporated into the benchmarking framework do not necessarily represent the entire metric universe for synthetic data assessment.

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=11&annotation=981R|Yan et al. 2022, p.11]])
> the introduced benchmarking framework was specifically designed for contrasting models that generate structured EHR data.

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=11&annotation=983R|Yan et al. 2022, p.11]])
> there is no clear consensus on what an acceptable privacy risk threshold for synthetic data is

> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=11&annotation=985R|Yan et al. 2022, p.11]])
> generative models may induce bias and fairness issues, such that subgroups of the population are not evenly well-generated



### Zentrales Zitat:
> ([[Yan et al. 2022.pdf#page=2&annotation=987R|Yan et al. 2022, p.2]])
> This result clearly illustrates why synthetic data generation models need to be systematically assessed in the context of their use case before their application.
